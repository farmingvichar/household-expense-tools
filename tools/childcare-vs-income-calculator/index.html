How the Childcare vs. Take-Home Pay Calculator Works

Deciding whether both parents should remain in the workforce comes down to more than comparing a gross salary against a daycare invoice. When you work, every dollar you earn is taxed first, while daycare and commuting expenses are paid with after-tax dollars.

This calculator strips away headline salary figures to reveal your True Net Financial Gain using four steps:

  1. Gross-to-Net Paycheck Conversion: Whether you enter an annual salary or an hourly wage, the tool subtracts your combined federal, FICA, and state tax withholding.
  2. Dependent Care FSA Tax Credit: If you contribute pre-tax dollars to an employer-sponsored Dependent Care FSA, the calculator adds back the federal and FICA taxes you save on those sheltered funds.
  3. Childcare & Hidden Work Expenses: Weekly daycare rates are normalized to exact monthly averages, combined with out-of-pocket work costs such as gas, transit passes, parking, and lunches.
  4. Effective Hourly Rate & Break-Even Math: Your remaining monthly net gain is divided by your actual monthly working hours to show what you truly earn per hour—alongside the minimum gross salary required just to break even.

Frequently Asked Questions

Childcare Cost & Budgeting FAQs

How much does full-time childcare cost in the US right now?

Full-time center-based childcare in the United States now exceeds $2,000 per month ($24,000+ per year) in many metro areas. In high-cost states, annual infant daycare routinely tops $22,000 to $28,000, making it larger than the average mortgage or rent payment.

How do I find my combined tax rate for this calculator?

Look at a recent paystub and divide your total taxes withheld (Federal Income Tax + Social Security + Medicare + State/Local Tax) by your Gross Pay for that period. For most middle-income US households, this combined rate falls between 18% and 25%.

Does using a Dependent Care FSA really save money on daycare?

Yes. A Dependent Care FSA lets you pay for eligible childcare using pre-tax payroll deductions. If you shelter $7,500 in a DCFSA and sit in a 22% tax bracket, you save $1,650 in taxes—effectively lowering your net childcare cost by $137.50 per month.

Why is my "True Effective Hourly Wage" so much lower than my hourly pay?

Your stated hourly wage is calculated before taxes and assumes zero cost to show up to work. Because childcare and commuting must be paid out of your after-tax paycheck, subtracting $2,000+ a month often reduces a $25–$30/hour job to $5–$10/hour in actual spendable cash.
