How we calculate

All math uses whole cents, so shares always add up to the exact bill. When a bill cannot divide evenly, the extra cents go to different people on different bills.

  • Equal: everyone pays the same share.
  • Percent: percentages must add up to 100%.
  • Fixed amounts: amounts must add up to the bill total.
  • Days / shares: share = bill × (your days ÷ everyone's days). Enter 0 to leave someone out.

Balance = what you paid − what you owe. A positive balance means you get money back. A negative balance means you pay. Then the person who owes the most pays the person owed the most, and so on until everyone is settled. That takes at most one payment fewer than the number of roommates.

Example: Alex pays a $150.00 electric bill for 3 roommates, split equally. Each owes $50.00. Alex paid $150.00, so Alex gets back $100.00. Sam and Jordan each pay Alex $50.00.

How to split household bills fairly

Most roommate fights are not about money. They are about surprises. Agree on the rules before the first bill arrives, write them down, and run the numbers in one place everyone can see.

Equal split vs. usage-based split

Expense Usually fair
Wi-Fi Equal
Electricity, gas, water Equal, or by days if someone moves in or out
Shared groceries Equal, or fixed amounts for what each person actually uses
Streaming and subscriptions Only people who use it

Equal splits are simplest and cause the fewest arguments. Switch to a custom split only when the difference is large and easy to prove, such as one person working from home with a space heater running all day.

When someone moves in or out mid-month

Use the days method. A roommate who lived there 10 of 30 days pays one third of what a full-month roommate pays. Count the actual days both sides agree on and keep the number in your group chat.

When rooms are different sizes

For rent, a common approach is to split by room size or features, for example 40/30/30. Use the percent method for that. Utilities are usually still split equally, since everyone shares the kitchen, heat and Wi-Fi.

How to ask for money without the awkwardness

Send the summary the day the bill arrives. Keep it factual: the bill, the share, the due date. The copy button produces a plain message you can paste straight into your group chat. Many roommates then settle by Venmo, Zelle or Cash App; those are separate apps, and this tool does not connect to them.

Frequently asked questions

Roommate Bill Splitter FAQs

Practical answers on splitting mid-month bills, handling late payers, and managing shared utility costs.

How do I split utilities when a roommate moves in or out mid-month?

Use the days method. Enter how many days each person lived there. Each person pays their share of the bill in proportion to those days.

Should Wi-Fi be split equally?

Usually yes. Everyone benefits from the connection, and usage is hard to measure. If someone works from home and needs a premium plan, you can agree on a custom percentage instead.

What if one roommate pays late?

Agree on a due date up front, send the summary the day the bill arrives, and settle within a few days. Whoever put the bill on their card should not carry the cost for weeks.

Why do the shares sometimes differ by one cent?

A bill like $100.00 cannot be divided evenly by three. The calculator gives the extra cent to one person and rotates it between bills, so the shares always add up to the exact total.

Does this tool store my bills?

Calculations run in your browser and your bill data is not sent to our servers. If you tick Save on this device, the data is kept only in your own browser storage.
