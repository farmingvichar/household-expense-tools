How to use this toilet paper math calculator

Grocery store aisles make toilet paper pricing deliberately hard to compare. One brand advertises 12 Mega Rolls, another sells 24 Double Rolls, and bulk club packs often list different sheet sizes altogether. To find the real winner before it goes in your cart, look at the fine print near the bottom front of each package for four numbers: total rolls, sheets per roll, ply count, and optional total square feet.

Enter the final pack price after any digital store coupon or subscribe-and-save discount. The calculator immediately runs three transparent unit-price formulas:

Cost per 100 sheets = (Final Price ÷ (Rolls × Sheets per Roll)) × 100

Ply-adjusted cost (per 100 ply-sheets) = (Final Price ÷ (Total Sheets × Ply)) × 100

Price per square foot = Final Price ÷ Total Square Feet (multiply by 100 for store shelf-tag cost per 100 sq ft)

Price per square foot vs. cost per 100 sheets

Cost per 100 sheets is a quick way to compare two packs when both rolls have identical sheet dimensions. However, manufacturers frequently reduce sheet width—a common form of shrinkflation where sheets narrow from 4.5 inches down to 3.8 or 4.0 inches while the sheet count stays the same.

When total square footage is printed on both packages (standard on most US retail packs), entering it gives you the most accurate comparison. Price per 100 square feet also matches the unit pricing format printed on many US supermarket shelf tags, making it easy to spot true bulk value.

Why "Mega" and "Super Mega" roll labels are misleading

Claims like "12 Mega Rolls = 48 Regular Rolls" are not governed by a single industry standard. Each manufacturer defines its own "regular roll" baseline—often using a very small legacy roll of 70 to 100 sheets. A 12-roll Mega pack from one brand can contain hundreds fewer sheets than a 12-roll Mega pack from another brand at the exact same shelf price. Ignoring roll nicknames and comparing total sheets, ply, and square footage eliminates that confusion.

Limitations

This calculator provides objective mathematical comparisons based only on the numbers you enter. It does not measure paper softness, tear strength, absorbency, septic safety, or brand preference—a slightly pricier plush roll may still be worth it if your household uses fewer sheets per visit. Retail benchmarks are general estimates for informational purposes only, and the tool does not automatically add sales tax, shipping, or live currency conversion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Toilet Paper Value FAQs

Practical answers on sheet math, square footage, ply value, and Mega Roll labels.

Is price per sheet or price per square foot better for comparing toilet paper?

Price per square foot (or per 100 sq ft) is the most accurate metric because brands often shrink individual sheet widths while keeping the sheet count high. When square footage is not printed on the package, cost per 100 sheets is the next best comparison.

What is a good price per 100 sheets or per square foot in the US?

For standard 2-ply toilet paper in the US, a strong stock-up deal is generally around $0.20 or less per 100 sheets, or under $1.50 per 100 sq ft ($0.015 per sq ft). Typical everyday retail pricing usually falls between $0.21 and $0.28 per 100 sheets, depending on brand and pack size.

How do you fairly compare 1-ply vs. 2-ply toilet paper?

Use the ply-adjusted cost per 100 ply-sheets. Because a 2-ply sheet contains two layers of paper, dividing pack price by total sheets multiplied by ply count puts 1-ply, 2-ply, and 3-ply rolls on a more balanced footing.

What does "12 Mega Rolls = 48 Regular Rolls" actually mean?

Roll labels like Double, Mega, and Super Mega are not standardized across brands. Manufacturers compare their mega roll to their own small regular roll baseline. Checking total sheets and total square feet printed on the front label removes the marketing guesswork.

Can I use this calculator to compare paper towel packs too?

Yes. Paper towels use the same unit math. Enter pack price, roll count, sheets per roll, and ply—or add total square footage to compare select-a-size rolls fairly against full-sheet rolls.
