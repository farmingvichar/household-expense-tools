How unit price calculation works

The calculator converts compatible package sizes into one shared unit and then divides each item's shelf price by its converted quantity: Unit Price = Total Price ÷ Quantity.

Results automatically match the units you enter—showing price per oz or lb for US weights, per fl oz or gal (US) for US liquids, per 100 g or kg for metric weights, per 100 ml or liter for metric liquids, and per item or pack for count goods. When rates are below 1.00, the tool displays three decimal places so small per-ounce differences stay accurate.

Real supermarket comparison examples

US weight (oz vs. lb): An 18 oz cereal box for $4.50 costs $0.250 / oz, while a 2 lb (32 oz) box for $7.36 costs $0.230 / oz. The 2 lb box saves $0.020 per oz (8% cheaper).

Metric weight (g vs. kg): A 500 g bag of rice for $2.20 equals $4.40 / kg, whereas a 1 kg bag for $3.90 equals $3.90 / kg—saving $0.50 per kg (11.4% cheaper).

Item count: A 12-count carton of eggs at $3.60 costs $0.300 / item, while an 18-count carton at $4.86 costs $0.270 / item—saving $0.030 per egg (10% cheaper).

Why store shelf unit tags can mislead

Mixed units on the same shelf: One brand's tag may list price per ounce while the neighboring size lists price per pound or per count, making a quick glance unreliable.

Net weight vs. drained weight: Canned beans, tuna, and jarred vegetables include packing liquid in net weight. Compare drained weight when brands pack differently.

Bulk spoilage traps: A family pack that is 10% cheaper per ounce still wastes money if part of it goes stale or spoils before you finish it.

Limitations

This calculator compares weight with weight (1 lb = 16 oz; 1 kg = 1,000 g; 1 oz = 28.35 g), volume with volume (1 US gal = 128 US fl oz; 1 liter = 1,000 ml), items with items, and packs with packs. It cannot compare weight with liquid volume because product density varies.

It does not convert currencies or factor in sales tax, product quality, or delivery fees. This tool does not provide financial, tax, legal, investment, or accounting advice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Grocery Unit Price & Package Comparison FAQs

Quick answers on calculating price per ounce, converting metric and US labels, and spotting genuine supermarket value.

How do you calculate unit price?

Divide the total package price by its total quantity in ounces, pounds, grams, milliliters, or count (Unit Price = Total Price ÷ Quantity). For example, an 18 oz box of cereal for $4.50 costs $0.250 per oz, while a 24 oz box for $5.76 costs $0.240 per oz.

How do I compare price per ounce (oz) against pound (lb) or fluid ounce (fl oz)?

Enter each item's price and size, then select oz, lb, fl oz (US), or gal (US). Because 1 lb equals 16 oz and 1 US gallon equals 128 US fl oz, the calculator converts compatible units automatically so you can compare the true unit rate side by side.

How do I calculate price per 100g or compare grams with kilograms?

Select g for both items to compare rates per 100 g, or pair g with kg to compare both per kg. The calculator uses exact metric conversion (1 kg = 1,000 g and 1 liter = 1,000 ml) to show which pack costs less and the exact savings percentage.

Does buying a larger or bulk package always have a lower unit price?

No. Store sales, digital coupons, and store-brand regular sizes often beat warehouse or family-size packs on price per oz or per 100 g. Larger packs also cost more in practice if perishable food spoils before you finish it.

Does this grocery unit price calculator save or transmit my data?

No. All calculations run locally in your browser without accounts, cookies, localStorage, or external server requests.
